🟢 Learning Unit 1: IT Service Management Basics

Theme 1: What is IT Service Management?

Source (ITIL 4 Definition)

Service Management: A set of specialized organizational capabilities for enabling value for customers in the form of services.

Simplified

💡 Simple: ITSM is the "rules, roles, and tools" that keep IT running smoothly. It's not just about fixing computers — it's about understanding what customers need and delivering it reliably.

Real-World Example

🏢 Example: When you log a ticket for a password reset, the entire system that tracks your request, assigns it to an agent, sends you updates, and closes the ticket when resolved — that's ITSM in action.

Why ITSM Matters in the Modern World

  • Services comprise the largest component of developed and developing economies
  • Almost all services today are IT-enabled
  • Technology advances (cloud, AI, blockchain) create new value opportunities
  • IT is a strategic capability and source of competitive advantage
  • Organisations must balance stability with operational agility

About ITIL 4

  • ITIL has led the ITSM industry for 30+ years
  • ITIL 4 reshapes ITSM in the context of customer experience, value streams, and digital transformation
  • Embraces Lean, Agile, and DevOps
  • Key components: the Service Value System (SVS) and the Four Dimensions Model

Discussion

  • Why is ITSM about value, not just technology?
  • What happens when ITSM is done poorly?
  • How does ITSM differ from IT support?

Source

  • Organisation: The formal structure, culture, and policies
  • People: The human element — skills, attitudes, and communication

Key Message

The complexity of organisations is growing. It's important that the way an organisation is structured and managed, as well as its roles, responsibilities, and systems of authority, is well defined and supports its overall strategy.
💡 Simple: The organisation is the skeleton; the people are the muscles.
🏦 Example: A bank has a strict hierarchy (Organisation). But its success depends on whether employees are skilled, motivated, and communicate well (People).

Key Considerations

  • Culture of trust and transparency
  • Leadership styles and communication
  • Skills, competencies, and knowledge
  • Roles and responsibilities
  • Breaking down organisational silos
  • Focus on value creation among all staff

Theme 2: Information and Technology

Source

  • Information: Data with meaning and context
  • Technology: The tools that process information

Key Message

When applied to the SVS, this dimension includes the information and knowledge necessary for the management of services, as well as the technologies required. It also incorporates the relationships between different components of the SVS.
💡 Simple: Information is the message; technology is the phone.
📊 Example: A CRM system (Technology) stores customer names and purchase histories (Information). This supports the "Customer Service" IT service.

Key Questions to Ask

  • What information is managed by the services?
  • What supporting information is needed to deliver services?
  • How will information assets be protected, archived, and disposed of?
  • Is the technology compatible with current architecture?
  • Does it raise compliance issues?
  • Does it align with strategy?
  • Do we have the right skills to support it?

Theme 4: Value Streams, Processes & PESTLE

Source

  • Value Stream: A series of steps an organisation undertakes to create and deliver products and services to consumers
  • Processes: A set of interrelated or interacting activities that transform inputs into outputs

Key Message

Applied to the organisation and its SVS, the value streams and processes dimension is concerned with how the various parts of the organisation work in an integrated and coordinated way to enable value creation.
💡 Simple: Value Stream = The journey. Process = The steps in the journey.
🍕 Example: Value Stream: Ordering a pizza → Delivery. Process: Taking the order, baking, dispatching.

The SVS describes how all components and activities of the organisation work together as a system to enable value creation.

Organisational Agility vs Resilience

  • Organisational Agility: The ability to move and adapt quickly, flexibly, and decisively to support internal changes
  • Organisational Resilience: The ability to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and adapt to incremental changes and sudden disruptions
Theme 2: The 7 ITIL Guiding Principles

Source

A guiding principle is a recommendation that guides an organisation in all circumstances, regardless of changes in its goals, strategies, type of work, or management structure.

Figure: The 7 Guiding Principles — your decision-making compass.

PrincipleSimple MeaningExample
1. Focus on ValueEverything must map to value for stakeholdersBefore adding a feature, ask: "Does this help customers?"
2. Start Where You AreDon't reinvent the wheelImprove existing processes rather than starting from scratch
3. Progress Iteratively with FeedbackTake small steps, get feedbackLaunch a beta version, get feedback, improve
4. Collaborate & Promote VisibilityWork together, be transparentShare dashboards with all teams
5. Think & Work HolisticallySee the big picture — no silosConsider how IT changes affect sales and HR
6. Keep It Simple & PracticalDon't overcomplicateUse simple tools if they work
7. Optimise & AutomateMake things faster and efficientAutomate password resets
🏥 Example: Improving a slow helpdesk:
  1. Focus on value: Ask what customers want
  2. Start where you are: Use existing ticketing system
  3. Progress iteratively: Add a chatbot, test, get feedback
  4. Collaborate: Involve helpdesk staff in designing improvements
  5. Think holistically: Consider impact on other departments
  6. Keep it simple: Start with one improvement at a time
  7. Optimise & automate: Automate common requests
Theme 3: Governance, Value Chain & Continual Improvement

Source

Governance: The means by which an organisation is directed and controlled. Realised through Evaluate, Direct, and Monitor.

Governance Activities

  • Evaluate: Evaluate the organisation, its strategy, portfolios, and relationships
  • Direct: Assign responsibility, direct strategy and policies
  • Monitor: Monitor performance against policies and direction

The 6 Service Value Chain Activities

Figure: The 6 Value Chain Activities.

ActivityWhat It Does
PlanEnsure shared understanding of vision, current status, and improvement direction
ImproveEnsure continual improvement of products, services, and practices
EngageProvide good understanding of stakeholder needs and good relationships
Design & TransitionEnsure products and services meet expectations for quality, cost, time to market
Obtain & BuildEnsure service components are available when and where needed
Deliver & SupportEnsure services are delivered and supported according to agreed specs
⚠️ Common Rules for the Value Chain:
  • All external interactions are performed via Engage
  • All new resources are obtained through Obtain/Build
  • Planning at all levels is performed via Plan
  • Improvements at all levels are initiated and managed via Improve

The Continual Improvement Model

Figure: The 7-step Continual Improvement Model.

  1. What is the vision? — Define the initiative's vision and link to organisational goals
  2. Where are we now? — Assess the current state with objective measurement
  3. Where do we want to be? — Define target state with SMART objectives, CSFs, and KPIs
  4. How do we get there? — Plan the route, possibly with experiments
  5. Take action — Execute the plan, maintaining focus on measuring progress
  6. Did we get there? — Validate success and confirm value
  7. How do we keep the momentum going? — Embed and reinforce improvements

Q1. Define "Service Management" and explain its purpose in modern organisations. (5 marks)

Model Answer: Service Management is a set of specialized organizational capabilities for enabling value for customers in the form of services. Its purpose is to deliver value through services, align IT with business needs, ensure reliability, and continuously improve how services are delivered. In the modern world, almost all services are IT-enabled, making ITSM a strategic capability that provides competitive advantage.

Q2. List the Four Dimensions of Service Management and give one example of each. (8 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Organisations & People: Culture, structure, roles, skills (e.g., IT team, leadership)
  2. Information & Technology: Data, hardware, software, networks (e.g., servers, cloud platforms)
  3. Partners & Suppliers: External vendors and third parties (e.g., Microsoft, AWS)
  4. Value Streams & Processes: Workflows that turn inputs into value (e.g., incident resolution workflow)
Q3. Explain the difference between a Supplier and a Partner, with examples. (5 marks)

Model Answer: A Supplier provides goods or services under a contract, usually transactional. Example: A local IT shop for hardware repairs. A Partner has a strategic, long-term relationship with shared goals. Example: Microsoft for Office 365 with ongoing support and innovation. Partners involve deeper collaboration and co-creation of value, while suppliers are managed through contracts and SLAs.

Q4. Describe the purpose of the Continual Improvement Model and list its 7 steps. (10 marks)

Model Answer: The model provides a structured approach to implementing improvements. The 7 steps are:

  1. What is the vision?
  2. Where are we now?
  3. Where do we want to be?
  4. How do we get there?
  5. Take action
  6. Did we get there?
  7. How do we keep the momentum going?
Q5. Explain how Agile and ITIL can work together in an organisation. (5 marks)

Model Answer: Agile focuses on flexibility and rapid delivery, while ITIL provides structure and reliability. Together, they enable fast development with reliable operations. For example, a bank can use Agile to build a mobile app in 2-week sprints while ITIL ensures the infrastructure is secure and incidents are managed. Agile ITSM applies Agile principles to service management, such as using sprints to improve IT processes.

Q6. Describe two advantages of using AI in IT Service Management. (5 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Faster Resolution: AI resolves incidents up to 30.5% faster by automating routine tasks and suggesting solutions.
  2. Predictive Analytics: AI predicts and prevents incidents before they occur, such as detecting a server that will fail in 3 days.
Q7. Define "Value" in the context of service management and explain why it is subjective. (5 marks)

Model Answer: Value is the perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. It is subjective because different stakeholders perceive value differently. For example, a retired person might value flexibility in car hire, while a business might value reliability and cost-effectiveness. Value is co-created through the relationship between provider and consumer, and it changes over time and in different circumstances.

Q8. What is the purpose of the Service Value Chain? List its 6 activities. (7 marks)

Model Answer: The Service Value Chain is an operating model that outlines the key activities required to respond to demand and facilitate value realization through the creation and management of products and services. Its 6 activities are:

  1. Plan
  2. Improve
  3. Engage
  4. Design & Transition
  5. Obtain & Build
  6. Deliver & Support
Q9. Explain the difference between an Incident and a Problem. (5 marks)

Model Answer: An Incident is an unplanned interruption to a service or reduction in the quality of a service. It has an impact on users or business processes and must be resolved quickly to restore normal service operation. A Problem is a cause, or potential cause, of one or more incidents. Problems require investigation and analysis to identify the causes, develop workarounds, and recommend longer-term resolution. This reduces the number and impact of future incidents.

Q10. Describe the 7 ITIL Guiding Principles. (14 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Focus on Value: Everything must map to value for stakeholders.
  2. Start Where You Are: Don't reinvent the wheel — use what you have.
  3. Progress Iteratively with Feedback: Take small steps, get feedback, adjust.
  4. Collaborate & Promote Visibility: Work together and be transparent.
  5. Think & Work Holistically: See the big picture — no silos.
  6. Keep It Simple & Practical: Don't overcomplicate things.
  7. Optimise & Automate: Make things faster and more efficient.
Q11. What is the difference between Utility and Warranty? (5 marks)

Model Answer: Utility is the functionality offered by a product or service to meet a particular need. It can be summarised as "what the service does" and determines whether a service is "fit for purpose." Warranty is assurance that a product or service will meet agreed requirements. It can be summarised as "how the service performs" and determines whether a service is "fit for use." Warranty typically addresses availability, capacity, security, and continuity.

Q12. Explain the purpose of the Service Desk practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of the Service Desk practice is to capture demand for incident resolution and service requests. It should be the entry point and single point of contact for the service provider with all of its users. Service desks provide a clear path for users to report issues, queries, and requests, and have them acknowledged, classified, owned, and actioned.

Q13. Describe the 3 types of change in Change Control. (6 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Standard Changes: Low-risk, pre-authorised changes that are well understood and fully documented. Can be implemented without additional authorisation.
  2. Normal Changes: Changes that need to be scheduled, assessed, and authorised following a process. Change models determine the roles for assessment and authorisation.
  3. Emergency Changes: Changes that must be implemented as soon as possible, such as to resolve an incident or implement a security patch. The process for assessment and authorisation is expedited.
Q14. What is the purpose of Problem Management? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Problem Management is to reduce the likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual and potential causes of incidents, and managing workarounds and known errors. It involves three phases: Problem Identification, Problem Control, and Error Control.

Q15. Explain the concept of "Value Co-creation". (5 marks)

Model Answer: Value co-creation is the active collaboration between providers and consumers, as well as other organisations, to create value. Instead of the provider delivering value unilaterally, both parties work together to define requirements, design solutions, and create or provision the service. This ensures that the value delivered is truly aligned with what the consumer needs and wants.

Q16. List and explain 3 General Management Practices. (6 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Continual Improvement: Aligns practices and services with changing business needs through ongoing improvement.
  2. Information Security Management: Protects the information needed by the organisation to conduct its business.
  3. Project Management: Ensures all projects are successfully delivered through planning, delegating, monitoring, and maintaining control.
Q17. Describe the purpose of Availability Management. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Availability Management is to ensure that services deliver agreed levels of availability to meet the needs of customers and users. It involves negotiating achievable targets, designing infrastructure and applications for required availability, monitoring and reporting on availability, and planning improvements.

Q18. Explain the concept of "Known Error". (4 marks)

Model Answer: A Known Error is a problem that has been analysed but has not been resolved. It usually means that faulty components have been identified. Workarounds are documented for known errors so that future incidents can be resolved quickly. The problem remains in the known error status until a permanent resolution is found.

Q19. What is the purpose of Service Level Management? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Service Level Management is to set clear business-based targets for service levels, and to ensure that delivery of services is properly assessed, monitored, and managed against these targets. It provides end-to-end visibility of the organisation's services and ensures that the organisation meets the defined service levels.

Q20. Describe the 5 components of the SVS. (10 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Guiding Principles: Recommendations that guide an organisation in all circumstances.
  2. Governance: The means by which an organisation is directed and controlled.
  3. Service Value Chain: A set of interconnected activities that an organisation performs to deliver a valuable product or service.
  4. Practices: Sets of organisational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective.
  5. Continual Improvement: A recurring organisational activity performed at all levels to ensure performance continually meets stakeholders' expectations.
Q21. Explain the difference between "Output" and "Outcome". (5 marks)

Model Answer: An Output is a tangible or intangible deliverable of an activity. An Outcome is a result for a stakeholder enabled by one or more outputs. For example, one output of a wedding photography service may be an album. The outcome is the preservation of memories and the ability of the couple and their family to recall those memories.

Q22. Describe the purpose of the Service Catalogue Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Service Catalogue Management is to provide a single source of consistent information on all services and service offerings, and to ensure that it is available to the relevant audience. The service catalogue represents those services which are currently available and is a subset of the total list of services tracked in the service provider's service portfolio.

Q23. Explain the purpose of the Continual Improvement practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of the Continual Improvement practice is to align the organisation's practices and services with changing business needs through the ongoing improvement of products, services, and practices, or any element involved in the management of products and services. It involves encouraging continual improvement, securing time and budget, identifying and logging opportunities, assessing and prioritising, and making business cases.

Q24. What is the difference between "Service Provision" and "Service Consumption"? (5 marks)

Model Answer: Service Provision includes the management of the provider's resources, configured to deliver the service; ensuring access to these resources for users; fulfilment of the agreed service actions; service level management; and continual improvement. Service Consumption includes the management of the consumer's resources needed to use the service; service actions performed by users; and the receiving (acquiring) of goods.

Q25. Describe the purpose of the Information Security Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Information Security Management is to protect the information needed by the organisation to conduct its business. This includes understanding and managing risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information, as well as authentication and non-repudiation. Security is established through policies, processes, behaviours, risk management, and controls that balance prevention, detection, and correction.

Q26. Explain the "Watermelon SLA" effect. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The "Watermelon SLA" effect describes a situation where an SLA appears green (successful) on the outside but is actually red (failing) on the inside. This happens when SLAs are based on individual metrics like system availability (99.9%) without a business context. The reports may show success, but if unavailability occurs during critical business times, customer satisfaction will be low. The provider thinks it's doing a great job, but customers are dissatisfied.

Q27. Describe the purpose of the Risk Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Risk Management is to ensure that the organisation understands and effectively handles risks. Managing risk is essential to ensuring ongoing sustainability and creating value. Risks need to be identified, assessed, and treated. The practice also recognises that risk is associated with opportunity, and failure to take opportunities can be a risk itself.

Q28. What are the 6 activities of the Service Value Chain? (6 marks)

Model Answer:

  1. Plan
  2. Improve
  3. Engage
  4. Design & Transition
  5. Obtain & Build
  6. Deliver & Support
Q29. Explain the concept of "Minimum Viable Product" (MVP). (4 marks)

Model Answer: A Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is a version of the final product which allows the maximum amount of validated learning with the least effort. It has just enough features to satisfy early customers and provide feedback for future product development. The MVP concept is used in iterative development to test assumptions and gather feedback early.

Q30. Describe the purpose of the Deployment Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Deployment Management is to move new or changed hardware, software, documentation, processes, or any other component to live environments. It may also be involved in deploying components to other environments for testing or staging. Deployment works closely with release management and change control but is a separate practice.

Q31. What is the purpose of the Relationship Management practice? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Relationship Management is to establish and nurture the links between the organisation and its stakeholders at strategic and tactical levels. It includes the identification, analysis, monitoring, and continual improvement of relationships with and between stakeholders. It ensures stakeholders' needs are understood, satisfaction is high, and conflicting requirements are mediated appropriately.

Q32. Explain the concept of "Organisational Agility". (4 marks)

Model Answer: Organisational agility is the ability of an organisation to move and adapt quickly, flexibly, and decisively to support internal changes. These might include changes to the scope of the organisation, mergers and acquisitions, changing organisational practices, or technologies requiring different skills or organisational structure. It is essential for success in a constantly changing environment.

Q33. Describe the purpose of the IT Asset Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of IT Asset Management is to plan and manage the full lifecycle of all IT assets to help the organisation maximize value, control costs, manage risks, support decision-making about purchase, re-use, retirement, and disposal of assets, and meet regulatory and contractual requirements. It typically includes software, hardware, networking, cloud services, and client devices.

Q34. Explain the "Theory of Constraints" in the context of continual improvement. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The Theory of Constraints (ToC) states that the weakest link in the value chain determines the flow and throughput of the system. When planning improvements, it is crucial to focus on the highest-priority work — the weakest link. The weakest link must be elevated as much as possible (sometimes revealing a new weakest link), and all other steps in the value chain must be organized around it.

Q35. What is the purpose of the Service Request Management practice? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Service Request Management is to support the agreed quality of a service by handling all pre-defined, user-initiated service requests in an effective and user-friendly manner. Service requests are a normal part of service delivery and are not a failure or degradation of service. They can usually be formalized with clear, standard procedures for initiation, approval, fulfilment, and management.

Q36. Describe the purpose of the Service Continuity Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Service Continuity Management is to ensure that the availability and performance of a service are maintained at sufficient levels in case of a disaster. It provides a framework for building organisational resilience with the capability of producing an effective response that safeguards the interests of key stakeholders and the organisation's reputation, brand, and value-creating activities.

Q37. Explain the difference between "Emergency Change" and "Normal Change". (5 marks)

Model Answer: Emergency Changes must be implemented as soon as possible, such as to resolve an incident or implement a security patch. They are not typically included in a change schedule, and the process for assessment and authorisation is expedited. Normal Changes need to be scheduled, assessed, and authorised following a process. Change models based on the type of change determine the roles for assessment and authorisation.

Q38. What is the purpose of the Service Design practice? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Service Design is to design products and services that are fit for purpose, fit for use, and that can be delivered by the organisation and its ecosystem. This includes planning and organizing people, partners and suppliers, information, communication, technology, and practices for new or changed products and services, and the interaction between the organisation and its customers.

Q39. Explain the concept of "Design Thinking". (5 marks)

Model Answer: Design Thinking is a practical and human-centred approach that accelerates innovation. It is used to solve complex problems and find practical, creative solutions that meet the needs of the organisation and its customers. It includes activities such as inspiration and empathy, ideation (divergent and convergent thinking), prototyping, implementation, and evaluation. It is best applied by multi-disciplinary teams.

Q40. Describe the purpose of the Monitoring and Event Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Monitoring and Event Management is to systematically observe services and service components, and record and report selected changes of state identified as events. It identifies and prioritizes infrastructure, services, business processes, and information security events, and establishes the appropriate response to those events, including responding to conditions that could lead to potential faults or incidents.

Q41. What is the purpose of the Service Configuration Management practice? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Service Configuration Management is to ensure that accurate and reliable information about the configuration of services, and the configuration items (CIs) that support them, is available when and where it is needed. This includes information on how CIs are configured and the relationships between them. It helps the organisation understand how the many CIs that contribute to each service work together.

Q42. Explain the "Watermelon SLA" effect and how to avoid it. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The Watermelon SLA effect occurs when SLAs appear successful (green) but customers are actually dissatisfied (red). This happens when SLAs are based on single-system metrics without business context. To avoid it, Service Level Management must identify metrics that truly reflect customer experience and satisfaction. SLAs should relate to defined outcomes, include balanced bundles of metrics (e.g., customer satisfaction + key business outcomes), and be based on genuine agreement between provider and consumer.

Q43. Describe the purpose of the Supplier Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Supplier Management is to ensure that the organisation's suppliers and their performances are managed appropriately to support the seamless provision of quality products and services. This includes creating closer, more collaborative relationships with key suppliers to uncover and realize new value and reduce the risk of failure. It involves maintaining a supplier strategy, negotiating contracts, managing relationships, and monitoring performance.

Q44. Explain the concept of "Organisational Resilience". (4 marks)

Model Answer: Organisational resilience is the ability of an organisation to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and adapt to both incremental changes and sudden disruptions from an external perspective. External influences could be political, economic, social, technological, legal, or environmental. Resilience cannot be achieved without a common understanding of the organisation's priorities and objectives.

Q45. What is the purpose of the Workforce and Talent Management practice? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Workforce and Talent Management is to ensure that the organisation has the right people with the appropriate skills and knowledge and in the correct roles to support its business objectives. It covers a broad set of activities including planning, recruitment, onboarding, learning and development, performance measurement, and succession planning. It plays a critical role in establishing organisational velocity.

Q46. Describe the purpose of the Portfolio Management practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Portfolio Management is to ensure that the organisation has the right mix of programmes, projects, products, and services to execute the organisation's strategy within its funding and resource constraints. It involves developing a systematic framework, evaluating and prioritising proposals, implementing investment appraisal, analysing and tracking investments, and monitoring performance.

Q47. Explain the concept of "Customer Experience" (CX) and "User Experience" (UX). (5 marks)

Model Answer: Customer Experience (CX) is the entirety of the interactions a customer has with an organisation and its products. It can determine how the customer feels about the organisation. CX is both objective (e.g., delivery time) and subjective (e.g., website design). User Experience (UX) looks specifically at the ease of use of the product or service and how the customer interacts with it. Both are essential to ensuring products and services deliver desired value.

Q48. What is the purpose of the Business Analysis practice? (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Business Analysis is to analyse a business or some element of it, define its associated needs, and recommend solutions to address these needs and/or solve a business problem, which must facilitate value creation for stakeholders. It enables an organisation to communicate its needs in a meaningful way, express the rationale for change, and design and describe solutions that enable value creation.

Q49. Describe the purpose of the Measurement and Reporting practice. (5 marks)

Model Answer: The purpose of Measurement and Reporting is to support good decision-making and continual improvement by decreasing levels of uncertainty. This is achieved through the collection of relevant data on various managed objects and the valid assessment of this data in an appropriate context. Managed objects include products and services, practices, teams, suppliers, and the organisation as a whole.

Q50. Explain the concept of "Continual Improvement" and its relationship with the SVS. (5 marks)

Model Answer: Continual Improvement is a recurring organisational activity performed at all levels to ensure that an organisation's performance continually meets stakeholders' expectations. It is one of the key components of the ITIL SVS, supporting every other component. The ITIL continual improvement model provides a structured approach, and the "Improve" value chain activity embeds continual improvement into the value chain. It is not just a practice but a culture that must be embedded into every fibre of the organisation.
